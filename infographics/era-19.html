Early Cold War Era

1945 – 1960s
The U.S. and USSR settle into a global ideological standoff that shapes every facet of American life.
Big Idea
Containment of communism became the organizing principle of U.S. foreign policy — driving foreign aid (Marshall Plan), military alliances (NATO), proxy wars (Korea), nuclear arms racing, and a domestic anti-communist crusade.

Key Terms

  • Iron Curtain (Churchill, 1946)
  • Containment — Kennan's strategy
  • Truman Doctrine 1947
  • Marshall Plan — European recovery
  • NATO 1949 / Warsaw Pact
  • NSC-68 — Cold War blueprint
  • Domino theory
  • McCarthyism — anti-communist witch hunt
  • HUAC & Hollywood blacklist
  • Sputnik 1957 — space race
  • Military-industrial complex (Eisenhower)
  • Massive retaliation / Brinkmanship

Key People

  • Harry S. Truman — containment, Korea
  • Dwight D. Eisenhower — moderate Republican
  • John F. Kennedy — Bay of Pigs, Cuban Missile Crisis
  • Joseph McCarthy — anti-communist senator
  • George Kennan — "Long Telegram," containment
  • John Foster Dulles — Sec. of State, brinkmanship
  • Richard Nixon — VP, Kitchen Debate
  • Joseph Stalin / Nikita Khrushchev — Soviet leaders
  • Julius & Ethel Rosenberg — executed for spying

Key Events

  • Yalta & Potsdam Conferences 1945
  • Truman Doctrine 1947
  • Marshall Plan 1948
  • Berlin Airlift 1948–49
  • NATO formed 1949
  • USSR tests A-bomb / China falls to communism 1949
  • Korean War 1950–53
  • Army-McCarthy Hearings 1954
  • Sputnik launched 1957
  • U-2 incident 1960
  • Bay of Pigs 1961
  • Cuban Missile Crisis Oct 1962
Legacy & Connection
Cold War priorities shape U.S. policy through 1991, justify Vietnam, and supercharge defense spending and the surveillance state.
