World War II Era

1941 – 1945
Global war transforms the U.S. into a military and economic superpower.
Big Idea
The U.S. mobilized its industrial might to defeat the Axis powers, ended the Depression, became a superpower, and ushered in the atomic age — while the contradiction between fighting fascism abroad and racism at home set up the postwar civil rights movement.

Key Terms

  • Lend-Lease Act 1941
  • Pearl Harbor — surprise attack Dec 7, 1941
  • Executive Order 9066 — Japanese internment
  • Korematsu v. United States 1944
  • Rosie the Riveter — women in war work
  • Double V Campaign — victory abroad & at home
  • Tuskegee Airmen / Navajo Code Talkers
  • Island hopping — Pacific strategy
  • D-Day — Normandy invasion Jun 6, 1944
  • Manhattan Project — atomic bomb
  • Holocaust — Nazi genocide of 6 million Jews
  • GI Bill 1944

Key People

  • Franklin D. Roosevelt — wartime president
  • Harry S. Truman — succeeded FDR, dropped atomic bombs
  • Dwight D. Eisenhower — Allied commander, Europe
  • Douglas MacArthur — Pacific commander
  • George Marshall — Army Chief of Staff
  • J. Robert Oppenheimer — Manhattan Project
  • Adolf Hitler / Hideki Tojo / Mussolini — Axis leaders
  • Winston Churchill / Joseph Stalin — Allies
  • A. Philip Randolph — pushed FDR on jobs equality

Key Events

  • Pearl Harbor Dec 7, 1941
  • Executive Order 9066 Feb 1942
  • Battle of Midway Jun 1942
  • Stalingrad / N. Africa campaign 1942–43
  • D-Day (Normandy) Jun 6, 1944
  • Yalta Conference Feb 1945
  • FDR dies; Truman becomes president Apr 1945
  • V-E Day May 8, 1945
  • Hiroshima & Nagasaki Aug 6 & 9, 1945
  • V-J Day Sep 2, 1945
Legacy & Connection
The U.S. emerges as one of two superpowers — confronting the USSR in the Cold War — and the atomic age begins.
