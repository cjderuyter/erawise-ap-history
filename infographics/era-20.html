Civil Rights Movement

1950s – 1960s
Mass nonviolent protest forces the federal government to dismantle Jim Crow segregation.
Big Idea
Building on decades of legal battles and grassroots organizing, the civil rights movement won landmark Supreme Court rulings, ended legal segregation, and produced the Civil Rights Act (1964) and Voting Rights Act (1965).

Key Terms

  • Brown v. Board of Education — overturned Plessy 1954
  • "Massive resistance" — Southern white pushback
  • Sit-ins — Greensboro, NC 1960
  • Freedom Rides 1961
  • Civil disobedience
  • SCLC, SNCC, CORE, NAACP
  • "I Have a Dream" speech 1963
  • Civil Rights Act of 1964
  • Voting Rights Act of 1965
  • 24th Amendment — banned poll taxes 1964
  • Black Power / Black Panthers

Key People

  • Martin Luther King Jr. — SCLC, nonviolent leader
  • Rosa Parks — Montgomery Bus Boycott
  • Thurgood Marshall — argued Brown; first Black SCOTUS justice
  • Earl Warren — Chief Justice, liberal court
  • Malcolm X — Nation of Islam, Black self-determination
  • John Lewis — SNCC, Selma
  • Lyndon B. Johnson — Great Society, signed CRA & VRA
  • Stokely Carmichael — Black Power
  • Fannie Lou Hamer — MFDP, voting rights
  • Medgar Evers — NAACP, assassinated 1963

Key Events

  • Brown v. Board of Education 1954
  • Emmett Till murdered 1955
  • Montgomery Bus Boycott 1955–56
  • Little Rock Nine 1957
  • Greensboro Sit-ins 1960
  • Freedom Rides 1961
  • Birmingham campaign 1963
  • March on Washington Aug 1963
  • Civil Rights Act 1964
  • Selma to Montgomery / "Bloody Sunday" 1965
  • Voting Rights Act 1965
  • MLK assassinated Apr 1968
Legacy & Connection
Inspires later movements (women's, LGBTQ+, disability, Latino/Chicano). Voting rights, however, remain politically contested into the 21st century.
