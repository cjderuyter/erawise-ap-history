War on Terror Era

2001 – 2010s
9/11 redirects U.S. policy toward counterterrorism — and two long wars in the Middle East.
Big Idea
The 9/11 attacks reshaped U.S. foreign and domestic policy: two wars, a vast new security state, expanded surveillance, and lasting debates about the balance between liberty and security — alongside the worst economic crisis since the Depression.

Key Terms

  • 9/11 — terrorist attacks Sep 11, 2001
  • War on Terror
  • USA PATRIOT Act 2001
  • Department of Homeland Security 2002
  • Axis of Evil (Bush)
  • Weapons of Mass Destruction (WMDs)
  • Guantánamo Bay — detention camp
  • "Surge" in Iraq 2007
  • Great Recession 2007–09
  • TARP / Bank Bailout 2008
  • Affordable Care Act ("Obamacare") 2010
  • Tea Party movement
  • Arab Spring 2011

Key People

  • George W. Bush — 43rd president, two wars
  • Dick Cheney — VP, hawkish on Iraq
  • Donald Rumsfeld — Sec. of Defense
  • Colin Powell — Sec. of State, UN speech
  • Barack Obama — first Black president 2009–17
  • Hillary Clinton — Sec. of State
  • John McCain / Mitt Romney — GOP nominees
  • Osama bin Laden — killed by SEALs 2011
  • Edward Snowden — NSA leaker 2013

Key Events

  • 9/11 attacks Sep 11, 2001
  • U.S. invades Afghanistan Oct 2001
  • Patriot Act / DHS created 2001–02
  • Iraq War begins Mar 2003
  • Hurricane Katrina Aug 2005
  • Financial collapse / TARP Sep 2008
  • Obama elected Nov 2008
  • Affordable Care Act passes 2010
  • Bin Laden killed May 2011
  • Snowden leaks 2013
  • Same-sex marriage legalized (Obergefell) 2015
Legacy & Connection
Long wars, surveillance, and economic crisis fed populist anger on both left and right — setting the stage for the political upheavals of the 2010s.
