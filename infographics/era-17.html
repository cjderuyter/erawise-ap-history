Great Depression & New Deal

1929 – 1941
Economic collapse leads to the most sweeping expansion of federal power in American history.
Big Idea
FDR's New Deal redefined the relationship between citizens and the federal government, creating a social safety net (Social Security), regulating finance, and reshaping the Democratic coalition for decades.

Key Terms

  • Black Tuesday — Oct 29, 1929
  • Hoovervilles — shantytowns
  • Dust Bowl — ecological disaster
  • Bonus Army — WWI veterans' march, 1932
  • Hundred Days — FDR's first burst of legislation
  • Alphabet agencies: CCC, AAA, TVA, NRA, WPA, FDIC, SEC, SSA
  • Fireside Chats — radio addresses
  • Court-packing plan 1937
  • Social Security Act 1935
  • Wagner Act — protected union rights
  • Keynesian economics — deficit spending

Key People

  • Herbert Hoover — laissez-faire response
  • Franklin D. Roosevelt — 4-term president
  • Eleanor Roosevelt — activist First Lady
  • Frances Perkins — first female Cabinet member
  • Huey Long — "Share Our Wealth," critic of FDR
  • Father Coughlin — populist radio priest
  • John Steinbeck — The Grapes of Wrath
  • Dorothea Lange — Depression photographer

Key Events

  • Stock Market Crash Oct 1929
  • Smoot-Hawley Tariff 1930
  • Bonus Army marches on D.C. 1932
  • FDR elected; "Hundred Days" begin 1933
  • 21st Amendment ends Prohibition 1933
  • Social Security Act 1935
  • FDR landslide reelection 1936
  • Court-packing controversy 1937
  • "Roosevelt Recession" 1937–38
  • WWII begins in Europe 1939
Legacy & Connection
The New Deal coalition (urban workers, Black voters, South, immigrants) anchored Democratic power for 30+ years; New Deal programs still structure American life.
