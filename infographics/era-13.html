American Imperialism

1890 – 1914
The U.S. becomes a global empire — Cuba, Hawaii, the Philippines, Panama.
Big Idea
Following European examples and Mahan's naval theory, the U.S. acquired overseas territories through war, treaty, and intervention. The Spanish-American War of 1898 transformed America into a colonial power, and the Panama Canal cemented its global reach.

Key Terms

  • Imperialism — extending U.S. power abroad
  • Mahan's Influence of Sea Power 1890
  • Yellow journalism — Hearst & Pulitzer
  • USS Maine — "Remember the Maine!"
  • Spanish-American War — "splendid little war"
  • Treaty of Paris — U.S. gets Phil./PR/Guam 1898
  • Teller Amendment / Platt Amendment
  • Insular Cases — Constitution doesn't follow the flag
  • Open Door Policy (China)
  • "Big Stick" diplomacy (TR)
  • Roosevelt Corollary (1904)
  • Dollar Diplomacy (Taft)
  • Anti-Imperialist League
  • "White Man's Burden" (Kipling)

Legacy & Connection
The U.S. emerges as a Pacific and Caribbean power with strategic naval dominance — setting the stage for its 20th-century role as a superpower. The contradictions between democracy at home and empire abroad fuel decades of debate.
