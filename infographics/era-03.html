Colonial Era

1607 – 1754
Thirteen British colonies grow into distinctive regional societies under "salutary neglect."
Big Idea
English colonies developed three regional patterns (New England, Middle, Southern), built distinct economies (subsistence, mixed, plantation), and grew increasingly self-governing under "salutary neglect."

Key Terms

  • Joint-stock company — Virginia Company funded Jamestown
  • Headright system — land grants for transporting settlers
  • Indentured servitude — labor for passage
  • Mayflower Compact — self-government model 1620
  • Salutary neglect — loose British colonial enforcement
  • Triangular trade — Atlantic exchange (slaves, sugar, rum)
  • First Great Awakening — religious revival of 1730s–40s
  • Middle Passage — brutal Atlantic slave voyage

Key People

  • John Smith — Jamestown leader
  • John Winthrop — "City upon a Hill," Mass. Bay
  • Roger Williams — founded Rhode Island, religious freedom
  • Anne Hutchinson — banished for religious dissent
  • William Penn — Quaker, founded Pennsylvania
  • Jonathan Edwards & George Whitefield — Great Awakening preachers
  • Nathaniel Bacon — led 1676 rebellion in Virginia

Key Events

  • Jamestown founded 1607
  • First Africans arrive in Virginia 1619
  • House of Burgesses meets 1619
  • Plymouth founded 1620
  • Pequot War 1636–38
  • King Philip's War 1675–76
  • Bacon's Rebellion 1676
  • Glorious Revolution / colonial impact 1688–89
  • Salem Witch Trials 1692
  • Stono Rebellion (slave revolt, SC) 1739
  • First Great Awakening peaks 1730s–40s
  • Era ends as French & Indian War begins 1754
Legacy & Connection
A century and a half of "salutary neglect" let the colonies develop their own representative assemblies, economies, and identities — the foundation that would make rebellion possible when Britain tightened its grip.
