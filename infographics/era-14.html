Progressive Era

1890s – 1920
Reformers tackle the abuses of industrial capitalism, expand democracy, and transform American government.
Big Idea
Middle-class reformers, muckraking journalists, and three reform-minded presidents (TR, Taft, Wilson) tamed corporate power, regulated industry, expanded suffrage, and produced four constitutional amendments (16th–19th).

Key Terms

  • Muckrakers — investigative journalists
  • Trust-busting — breaking up monopolies
  • Square Deal (TR) / New Freedom (Wilson)
  • 16th Amend. — income tax
  • 17th Amend. — direct election of senators
  • 18th Amend. — Prohibition
  • 19th Amend. — women's suffrage 1920
  • Initiative, Referendum, Recall
  • Settlement houses (Hull House)
  • Eugenics & scientific racism — dark side

Key People

  • Theodore Roosevelt — trust buster, conservation
  • William Howard Taft — successor, more trusts busted
  • Woodrow Wilson — Federal Reserve, Clayton Act
  • Jane Addams — Hull House, settlement movement
  • Upton Sinclair — The Jungle (meatpacking)
  • Ida Tarbell — exposed Standard Oil
  • Jacob Riis — How the Other Half Lives
  • W.E.B. Du Bois — NAACP, Niagara Movement
  • Booker T. Washington — Atlanta Compromise
  • Alice Paul & Carrie Chapman Catt — suffrage

Key Events

  • Spanish-American War / acquisition of Phil. 1898
  • NAACP founded 1909
  • Triangle Shirtwaist Fire 1911
  • 16th & 17th Amendments 1913
  • Federal Reserve Act 1913
  • Clayton Antitrust Act 1914
  • Pure Food & Drug Act, Meat Inspection Act 1906
  • 18th Amendment (Prohibition) 1919
  • 19th Amendment (Women's vote) 1920
Legacy & Connection
The expanded role of federal government becomes the foundation for the New Deal — and the modern regulatory state.
