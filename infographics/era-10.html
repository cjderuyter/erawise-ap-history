Civil War Era

1861 – 1865
The bloodiest conflict in American history ends slavery and redefines the Union.
Big Idea
A four-year war between Union and Confederacy preserved the United States, abolished slavery via the 13th Amendment, and reshaped the federal government's relationship to the states and to individual rights.

Key Terms

  • Union (North) vs. Confederacy (South)
  • Anaconda Plan — Union strategy to squeeze South
  • Emancipation Proclamation Jan 1, 1863
  • Habeas corpus suspended
  • Conscription / Draft — riots in NYC 1863
  • Total war — Sherman's tactics
  • 54th Massachusetts — African American Union regiment
  • Gettysburg Address 1863
  • 13th Amendment — abolished slavery 1865

Key People

  • Abraham Lincoln — Union president
  • Jefferson Davis — Confederate president
  • Robert E. Lee — Confederate general
  • Ulysses S. Grant — Union general
  • William T. Sherman — March to the Sea
  • "Stonewall" Jackson — Confederate general
  • Frederick Douglass — pushed Lincoln on emancipation
  • Clara Barton — battlefield nurse
  • John Wilkes Booth — assassin

Key Events

  • Fort Sumter — war begins Apr 1861
  • 1st Bull Run 1861
  • Antietam (bloodiest single day) Sep 1862
  • Emancipation Proclamation Jan 1863
  • Gettysburg & Vicksburg (turning point) Jul 1863
  • Gettysburg Address Nov 1863
  • Sherman's March to the Sea 1864
  • Lee surrenders at Appomattox Apr 9, 1865
  • Lincoln assassinated Apr 14, 1865
  • 13th Amendment ratified Dec 1865
Legacy & Connection
~620,000–750,000 deaths. Slavery ends but Reconstruction must answer: what does freedom mean, and who gets full citizenship?
