Westward Expansion & Manifest Destiny

1820s – 1853
Pioneers, soldiers, and miners stretch the United States to the Pacific.
Big Idea
Driven by “Manifest Destiny” — the belief that Americans were divinely fated to spread across the continent — the U.S. acquired vast territory through migration, treaty, war, and gold rush. The result transformed the nation's geography, displaced Indigenous peoples and Mexicans, and supercharged the slavery debate.

Key Terms

  • Manifest Destiny — divine right to expand (term coined 1845)
  • Oregon Trail — 2,000-mile pioneer route to the Pacific NW
  • "54° 40′ or Fight!" — Polk's Oregon stance vs. Britain
  • Texas Revolution / Republic of Texas (1836)
  • "Remember the Alamo!"
  • Mexican-American War (1846–48)
  • Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo — Mexican Cession 1848
  • Wilmot Proviso — ban slavery in new territory (failed)
  • Gadsden Purchase 1853
  • California Gold Rush & "Forty-Niners"
  • Mountain men / fur trade
  • Mormon migration to Utah

Key People

  • James K. Polk — expansionist president, "Young Hickory"
  • John L. O'Sullivan — coined "Manifest Destiny"
  • Sam Houston — led Texas independence; Texas pres.
  • Stephen F. Austin — "Father of Texas"
  • Davy Crockett & Jim Bowie — died at the Alamo
  • Antonio López de Santa Anna — Mexican leader
  • Brigham Young — led Mormons to Utah
  • John C. Frémont — "Pathfinder" explorer
  • Zachary Taylor & Winfield Scott — Mex.-Am. War U.S. generals
  • John Sutter — gold discovered on his land 1848
  • Henry David Thoreau — opposed war (Civil Disobedience)

Key Events

  • Santa Fe Trail opens 1821
  • American settlers begin Texas migration 1820s
  • Battle of the Alamo Mar 1836
  • Battle of San Jacinto — Texas independence Apr 1836
  • Trail of Tears 1838–39
  • Oregon Trail surge 1840s
  • Texas annexed by U.S. 1845
  • Mexican-American War begins May 1846
  • Bear Flag Revolt (California) 1846
  • Mormon migration to Salt Lake 1846–47
  • Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo Feb 1848
  • Gold discovered at Sutter's Mill Jan 1848
  • California Gold Rush peak 1849
  • California statehood 1850
  • Gadsden Purchase from Mexico 1853
Legacy & Connection
The Mexican Cession added 525,000+ square miles to the U.S., raising the explosive question of whether new territories would be free or slave. Compromise after compromise failed — setting the stage for the Sectional Crisis and Civil War.
