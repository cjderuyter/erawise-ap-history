6

Jacksonian Era

1824 – 1840s
The "common man" reshapes American democracy — for white men, at the expense of Native peoples.
Big Idea
Andrew Jackson's brand of populism expanded white male suffrage and executive power — while devastating Indigenous nations and igniting fierce battles over the national bank, nullification, and federal authority.

Key Terms

  • Universal white male suffrage — property requirements drop
  • Spoils system — political patronage
  • Kitchen Cabinet — Jackson's informal advisors
  • Bank War — Jackson kills 2nd Bank of U.S.
  • Nullification — states can void federal laws (Calhoun)
  • Indian Removal Act 1830
  • Trail of Tears — forced removal of Cherokee
  • Whig Party — anti-Jackson opposition
  • "King Andrew" — critics' nickname

Key People

  • Andrew Jackson — 7th president, populist
  • John C. Calhoun — VP, nullification theorist
  • Henry Clay — "American System," Whig
  • Daniel Webster — orator, defender of Union
  • Martin Van Buren — Jackson's heir, Panic of 1837
  • John Quincy Adams — lost "Corrupt Bargain" of 1824
  • Nicholas Biddle — Bank president

Key Events

  • "Corrupt Bargain" election 1824
  • Jackson elected 1828
  • Indian Removal Act 1830
  • Worcester v. Georgia 1832 (Jackson defies court)
  • Nullification Crisis 1832–33
  • Bank War / Pet Banks 1832–36
  • Trail of Tears 1838–39
  • Panic of 1837
Legacy & Connection
Set patterns of strong presidential power and party politics. The nullification debate previews the slavery-driven sectional conflict to come.
